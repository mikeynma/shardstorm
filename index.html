The game: rocks, a saucer, three lives

Turn, thrust and fire round a black playfield that wraps at every edge. Rocks split into smaller rocks when shot, and from wave 2 a saucer comes hunting once the rocks are few. Three lives, and a high score to beat.

The border does the showing off

The playfield is stark black, so the border has the colour: a ticker scrolling across the top, bars along the bottom that move to the music, a flash while you thrust, and between waves a set piece - a checkerboard, side stripes or a cloudy sea.

The tape: play while it loads

Load the tape and it plays a jingle, the loading screen develops like a photo, and then a small Shardstorm plays in the screen's box while the game loads - with the tune on a 128K. Your best score becomes the game's high score.

Loading it

Load the tape

Six files: a tape for each machine, each in three forms. Pick the .tzx if your emulator plays tapes in real time.

Tapes last built: 20 November 2026.

  1. Pick your machine's tape. The 48K tape for a 48K; the 128K tape for a 128K, +2, +2A or +3 (pick Tape Loader, or Loader on a +2A or +3). Each says so if you load it on the other. The loader in the .tzx and .tap has been checked against the 48K's and the 128K's timing; on a +2A or +3, whose timing differs a little, the -instant.tap is the safe one.
  2. The .tzx is the whole thing: a jingle on the tape, the loading screen developing, and a small game to play in its box while Shardstorm loads - with the tune on a 128K. Its own loader reads the tape, so the emulator must play it in real time, as ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine and Fuse do: switch off instant and fast loading. Press fire when it says LOADED.
  3. The .tap is the same without the jingle, for an emulator that only takes .tap files.
  4. The -instant.tap is the plain tape, every block for the ROM: for a DivMMC, or an emulator's instant loading, which skip any loader of its own.
  5. On a real Spectrum: play the .tzx into the EAR socket from a TZX player.

About

How it's made

Our own tools

An assembler and a Spectrum emulator written for this work, in JavaScript. The emulator models each machine's timing to the T-state, the 128K's paging and AY chip, and a tape deck that plays the real pulses.

Checked by playing

The build plays the game on a 48K, a 128K and a stand-in for the +2A/+3, and compares every screen it draws with what the game says should be there, pixel for pixel. The tapes are loaded pulse by pulse and the game in the box played to the end.

Then looked at

Every check leaves a screenshot, because a picture shows what a check can miss.

Shardstorm is an original game for the 48K and 128K ZX Spectrum, written in Z80 assembly with our own assembler and emulator. Made in the UK, by the maker of games for the ZX Spectrum Next, Borders & Loaders (how its tapes and border work) and the Spectrum Sound Lab.
