The border does the showing off

The playfield is stark black, so the border has the colour: a ticker scrolling across the top, bars along the bottom that move to the music, a flash while you thrust, and between waves a set piece - a checkerboard, side stripes or a cloudy sea.

A Spectrum's border is just one colour at a time. Change it at the right moment, though, and the TV draws the new colour from exactly that point on its way down the screen. Do that a few hundred times a frame, each change at an exact moment, and the border can carry pictures.

Shardstorm does that while the game runs: the top 24 lines of border hold a ticker with the wave, the high score to beat and messages such as NEW HIGH SCORE! and LAST LIFE!; on a 128K the bottom 24 hold bars that move to the tune; and between waves the whole border puts on a set piece for three seconds.

How it works

Timed to the T-state

Every colour change is timed to the processor's cycle, and the 48K and the 128K run at different speeds, so the border code is built twice, once for each, and the game loads the right one. The game's drawing has to fit round it, which is why the 48K, with only one screen, plays the lighter game.

On a +2A or +3

The bottom bars find the TV's beam by watching the bus for what the screen is fetching, which a +2A or +3 cannot do. Those two play without the bars and keep everything else.

Where it came from

The effects were tried out first in Border Lab on Borders & Loaders, the site about the border and tape tricks behind this game.

How it was checked

The build logs every write to the screen with its exact moment, and fails if one lands while the TV is drawing the playfield, or if a frame runs past its time on any of the three machines. It checks the ticker both with the interrupt line held as a real Spectrum holds it and without, as some emulators do.

Load it yourself

For the 48K and the 128K. A tape for each machine. The .tzx has it all: the jingle, the developing screen and the game in the box while it loads, and needs an emulator that plays tapes in real time. The .tap is the same without the jingle. The -instant.tap is the plain tape, for a DivMMC or instant loading.

  1. Pick your machine's tape. The 48K tape for a 48K; the 128K tape for a 128K, +2, +2A or +3 (pick Tape Loader, or Loader on a +2A or +3). Each says so if you load it on the other. The loader in the .tzx and .tap has been checked against the 48K's and the 128K's timing; on a +2A or +3, whose timing differs a little, the -instant.tap is the safe one.
  2. The .tzx is the whole thing: a jingle on the tape, the loading screen developing, and a small game to play in its box while Shardstorm loads - with the tune on a 128K. Its own loader reads the tape, so the emulator must play it in real time, as ZX Spin, Retro Virtual Machine and Fuse do: switch off instant and fast loading. Press fire when it says LOADED.
  3. The .tap is the same without the jingle, for an emulator that only takes .tap files.
  4. The -instant.tap is the plain tape, every block for the ROM: for a DivMMC, or an emulator's instant loading, which skip any loader of its own.
  5. On a real Spectrum: play the .tzx into the EAR socket from a TZX player.
